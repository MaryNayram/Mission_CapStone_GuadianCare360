CIA

Confidentiality: This means keeping patient information private. Only approved people — like doctors, nurses, or health workers — should be able to see or use it. It helps protect patients from embarrassment or misuse of their personal details. Example: A nurse at Korle Bu Teaching Hospital sends lab results to a doctor using a secure hospital app instead of WhatsApp. This keeps sensitive details like HIV status or pregnancy results away from outsiders.

Integrity: Integrity means making sure health records are correct and haven’t been changed by mistake or on purpose. Accurate data helps doctors give the right treatment and avoid errors. Example: A clinic in Tamale uses a digital system that checks for mistakes when entering patient details. If someone tries to change a prescription without permission, the system alerts the administrator.

Availability: Availability means patient records and hospital systems should be working and ready when needed. If they go down, it can delay treatment and put lives at risk. Example: A hospital in Kumasi keeps backup copies of patient files and has a generator to power their computers during dumsor (power outages). This ensures doctors can still access patient history during emergencies.

Understanding and applying these three principles — Confidentiality, Integrity, and Availability — helps protect patient trust, improve care, and keep health systems running smoothly. Every clinic, hospital, and health worker in Ghana should follow them to avoid data breaches, wrong treatments, or service delays.
